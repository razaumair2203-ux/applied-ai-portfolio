# Clear Run — October 2026 engineering status

**A multidisciplinary prototype and field-research programme; not a commercial or certified airport system.**

Clear Run addresses the complete foreign-object-debris response workflow: airborne RGB/LWIR detection, target localization, human-supervised ground-control tasking, a mobile collection vehicle, and confirmation of physical retrieval.

## Progress since the September portfolio snapshot

The ground collector has progressed from design/integration to **reported fabricated collection hardware with a functioning mechanism**. The team reports a later tracked/skid-steer version, but the retained manuscript photograph depicts a wheeled collector; without build identification those are separate, unresolved evidence baselines. The project lead reports a complete integrated mission demonstration, followed by additional day/night trial activity. The existing public September field-video/event extracts remain historical snapshots and should not be presented as the latest project state.

Research benchmarks, onboard inference, and the team-reported field campaign are different forms of evidence. The September mission-verification matrix is a historical stage record and has not been overwritten to represent October tests. In particular, prototype demonstrations do not by themselves establish repeatable field reliability, independently verified geolocation accuracy, safety approval, or readiness for deployment at an operational airport.

## Engineering contribution

My contribution is **programme-level technical direction and systems engineering**: defining interfaces, reviewing architecture and integration risks, setting experiment and verification priorities, establishing acceptance gates, and coordinating the multidisciplinary team. Flight operations, ground hardware, embedded software, and field trials are team outputs, not individual-authorship claims.

## What remains to be demonstrated quantitatively

- Ground-truth localization error and its variation across conditions.
- Traceable assignment, acknowledgement, and terminal approach.
- Capture and retention success with explicit task denominators, failures, and conditions.
- End-to-end identity and timestamp traceability, repeatability, and intervention rates.
